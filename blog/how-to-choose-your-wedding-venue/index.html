How to Choose Your Wedding Venue: Questions to Ask

A venue sets the tone of the whole day: how your guests arrive, where the ceremony feels intimate or grand, and whether you can relax once the program starts. The photos matter, but a short, thoughtful site visit will tell you more. Bring a notebook, go with someone who will be honest with you, and use the questions below as a starting point—not a test.

Before you go

Be clear on a few basics so you can compare places fairly. You do not need a final guest list, but a range helps. Know your preferred date or season, whether the ceremony and reception should be in one place, and what already matters to your families (access for elders, parking, a church nearby, or room for children).

Visit at a time of day close to your event if you can. Late afternoon light, midday heat, and evening lamps can change the same garden completely. Walk from the entrance to the ceremony area, then to the reception, the restrooms, and the parking, the way a guest would.

Space, guests, and flow

  • How many guests does this space hold comfortably for a ceremony, a reception, or both? Ask for a range, and picture your own family in it—not just a full room.
  • Can the ceremony and reception happen on the same grounds? If guests need to transfer, how long does that usually take?
  • Are there other events on the same day? If so, how do arrivals, sound, and parking stay separate?
  • Is there a quiet space for the couple, for family photos, or for sponsors to wait?
  • How accessible is the venue for older relatives, guests using a wheelchair, or families with small children?

Weather, timing, and comfort

In the Philippines, a rain plan is not a pessimistic thought. It is part of being a good host. Ask what happens if the weather turns, and look at that backup space yourself.

  • What is the indoor or covered option, and does it still fit your guest count?
  • What time can suppliers enter, and what is the curfew or end time for music?
  • Is there a generator or a backup if the power goes out?
  • How is the space cooled or shaded at the time of day you are considering?
  • Are restrooms enough for your guest count, and are they easy to find?

What is included—and what is not

Two venues can quote a similar package and still mean very different things. Ask for a written list of inclusions, then go through it line by line.

  • What furniture, lighting, and basic styling come with the venue? What would you need to rent?
  • Is catering in-house, limited to accredited suppliers, or open to a caterer you already love?
  • Are corkage, cake, and souvenir tables allowed, and are there extra fees?
  • Does the venue provide a coordinator on the day, and what do they handle versus what you must bring in?
  • What is the overtime rate, and when does it start?

Logistics and the neighborhood

  • How do guests get there? Ask about parking, a drop-off point, and whether a shuttle is useful for a church-to-venue transfer.
  • Is there a recommended nearby church or civil venue, and how long is the drive in typical traffic?
  • Where do suppliers load in, and is there a holding area for their equipment?
  • Are there rooms or nearby lodgings if family needs to stay close, or should you arrange that on your own?
  • What permits, if any, are you responsible for?

The agreement

Do not feel rushed to sign during a first visit. Take the contract home, read the cancellation and postponement terms, the payment schedule, and who is liable if something is damaged. If a promise was made on the tour—about setup time, a specific space, or a rain plan—ask to see it in writing.

  • How is the reservation confirmed, and what is refundable?
  • When is the final guest count due?
  • What happens if you need to change the date?
  • Which rules apply to music, drones, fireworks, or confetti?

A few notes after the visit

Before you leave, take photos of the spaces as they are, not only the styled corners. Jot down how you felt when you walked in: Did the staff have time for your questions? Did the grounds feel cared for? Could you imagine your parents and friends being comfortable there?

Compare no more than a few serious options. The right venue is not always the most decorated one. It is the place where your plan fits, your guests will be looked after, and you can stop worrying about the setting and start looking forward to the day.
